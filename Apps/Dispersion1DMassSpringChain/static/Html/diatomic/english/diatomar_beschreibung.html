Derivation of the Dispersion Relation

The equations of motion for the two masses $$m_1$$ and $$m_2$$ are given by:

$$m_1 \cdot \ddot{u}_n = c \, (v_{n-1} - 2 u_n + v_n)$$
$$m_2 \cdot \ddot{v}_n = c \,(u_{n} - 2 v_n + u_{n+1})$$

with the parameters:

  • Displacement of $$m_1$$: $$u_l$$
  • Displacement of $$m_2$$: $$v_l$$
  • Number of the unit cell $$n$$

Due to the periodicity of the system, the Bloch theorem can be used to derive the eigenvalue problem $$\omega(k)$$ based on a single unit cell. For this purpose, plane harmonic waves are assumed as solutions of the homogeneous differential equation system.
Approach:

$$u_n(t)=A(k(\omega))\text{e}^{\text{i}(ka-\omega t)}$$
$$v_n(t)=B(k(\omega))\text{e}^{\text{i}(ka-\omega t)}$$

$$k$$ is the Bloch wave vector and is defined as: $$k=\frac{2\pi n}{Na}$$.
$$n$$ describes the numbering of a unit cell, and $$N$$ is the total number of unit cells in a section of the periodic structure that fulfills the periodic boundary conditions using the Bloch theorem.
Furthermore, the definition of the dimensionless wave number $$\mu=\frac{2\pi}{Na}a$$ is adopted. Thus, the approach simplifies to:

$$u_n(t)=A(k(\omega))\text{e}^{\text{i}(\mu n-\omega t)}$$,
$$v_n(t)=B(k(\omega))\text{e}^{\text{i}(\mu n-\omega t)}$$.

Substituting into the linear differential equation system yields:

$$-\omega^2m_1A=-2cA+cB(1+\text{e}^{-\text{i}\mu})$$,
$$-\omega^2m_2B=cA(1+\text{e}^{\text{i}\mu})-2cB$$.

Or in matrix form:
$$\begin{bmatrix} \omega^2m_1-2c & c(1+\text{e}^{-\text{i}\mu}) \\ (1+\text{e}^{\text{i}\mu}) & \omega^2m_2-2c \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} A\\B \end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix} 0\\0 \end{bmatrix}$$

For the linear equation system, there exists a nontrivial solution ($$A\neq 0,B\neq 0$$) if the dynamic stiffness matrix $$[\mathbf{K}-\omega^2\mathbf{M}]$$ satisfies:
$$det(\mathbf{K}-\omega^2\mathbf{M})=0$$.
Hence, the dispersion relation $$\omega(k)$$ follows:

$$\omega_{+}$$ $$=\frac{c}{m_1m_2}\left(m_1+m_2+\sqrt{m_1^2+m_2^2+2m_1m_2\cos{(ka)}}\right)$$ $$=\frac{c}{m_1m_2}\left(m_1+m_2+\sqrt{m_1^2+m_2^2+2m_1m_2\cos{(\mu)}}\right)$$
$$\omega_{-} =\frac{c}{m_1m_2}\left(m_1+m_2-\sqrt{m_1^2+m_2^2+2m_1m_2\cos{(ka)}}\right) = \frac{c}{m_1m_2}\left(m_1+m_2-\sqrt{m_1^2+m_2^2+2m_1m_2\cos{(\mu)}}\right)$$

The low-frequency dispersion relation is referred to as the acoustic branch, and the higher frequency solution as the optical branch. In contrast to the monoatomic system, two normal modes exist for each wave vector $$k$$. An acoustic and an optical mode.
