Animation of the Diatomic Mass-Feder Chain

In the following, the normal modes of a diatomic mass-spring chain can be visualized. For this purpose, a segment of the structure consisting of $$N+1$$ unit cells has been connected using the periodic boundary conditions of the Bloch theorem. The invariant parameters of the animation are:

  • Mass $$m_2 = 1$$
  • Spring constant $$c = 1$$
  • Distance between unit cells $$a = 8$$
  • Number of visualized masses $$n = 32$$
  • Number of unit cells $$N = 16$$ (or number of normal modes)
To visualize the normal modes, the real part of $$u_n$$ and $$v_n$$ is calculated.
This is determined by the amplitude ratio $$\frac{A}{B}=\frac{c\,(1+\text{e}^{-\text{i}\mu})}{2c-\omega^2m_1}= \frac{2c-\omega^2m_2}{c\,(1+\text{e}^{\text{i}\mu})}$$.
For the acoustic branch, the real parts are given by:
  • $$u_n=\cos{(\mu n -\omega_{-}t)}$$
  • $$v_n=\frac{c}{2c-\omega^2_{-}m_2}((1+\cos{(\mu)})\cos{(\mu n-\omega_{-}t)} - \sin{(\mu)}\sin{(\mu n - \omega_{-}t)})$$

For the optical branch, they are given by:
  • $$u_n=\frac{c}{2c-\omega^2_{+}m_1}((1+\cos{(\mu)})\cos{(\mu n-\omega_{+}t)} + \sin{(\mu)}\sin{(\mu n - \omega_{+}t)})$$
  • $$v_n=\cos{(\mu n -\omega_{+}t)}$$

Using the & buttons, the values for $$\mu=ka$$ can be adjusted, and the corresponding normal modes can be visualized.
By clicking the , the frequency ranges in which the mass-spring chain does not oscillate can be visualized.
The button can be used to stop and start the animation.

Special Normal Modes:
At the boundaries of the reduced Brillouin zone $$[0;\pi]$$, the two extremes can be visualized.
For $$\mu=\pi$$, $$ \frac{\mathrm{d} \omega}{\mathrm{d} k} = 0$$ and:
  • in the acoustic branch, the heavy masses oscillate out of phase, while the light masses remain still
  • in the optical branch, the light masses oscillate out of phase, while the heavy masses remain still
For $$\mu = 0$$:
  • in the acoustic branch, all masses remain still
  • in the optical branch, $$ \frac{\mathrm{d} \omega}{\mathrm{d} k} = 0$$ and the light and heavy masses oscillate out of phase with the amplitude ratio $$\frac{u_k}{v_k}=\frac{-m_2}{m_1}$$
