Reminder: Fundamentals of Dispersion

The phase velocity is defined as:

$$\upsilon_P = \frac{\omega}{k}$$

The group velocity is defined as:

$$\upsilon_G = \frac{\mathrm{d} \omega}{\mathrm{d} k}$$

In a non-dispersive medium (such as light in a vacuum), it holds: $$ \frac{\mathrm{d} \omega}{\mathrm{d} k} = \frac{\omega}{k}$$. In this case, all waves propagate at the same constant speed regardless of their wavelength $$\lambda$$.
Consequently, all wave pulses maintain their form.
In contrast, in a dispersive medium: $$ \frac{\mathrm{d} \omega}{\mathrm{d} k} \neq \frac{\omega}{k}$$. In this case, the individual phases of a wave packet have a phase velocity that is dependent on their wave vector (or wavelength) and differs from the group velocity of the wave packet.
As a result, the individual phases of the wave packet propagate at different speeds and the shape of a single, isolated wave packet disperses over time.
For this reason, the ratio between $$\omega$$ and $$k$$ is referred to as the dispersion ratio.

Animation of Superposition of Two Normal Modes

With the aid of the following tool, two normal modes $$\mu_1=k_1a$$ and $$\mu_2=k_2a$$ within a frequency range from the reduced 1st Brillouin zone can be superimposed. The selected wave vectors are visualized in the above illustration.
The same experimental parameters as previously assumed are employed for the animation.
The displacement of the masses resulting from the superposition is visualised in an animation of the mass-spring chain. The graph below shows the displacements of the individual masses with respect to their rest position.
The x-axis corresponds to the positions of the initial positions of the individual masses. The equilibrium positions of the individual mass points are plotted in the graph as a blue point for the mass $$m_1$$ and as a gray point for the mass $$m_2$$. The y-axis corresponds to the longitudinal displacement of the masses. A positive displacement corresponds to a displacement of the mass to the right. The phase and group velocities of the superposition are depicted in the plot as a red and black point, respectively.
The solid blue ($$m_1$$) or gray line ($$m_2$$) represents the resulting wave from the superposition (hereafter referred to as the carrier wave), while the dashed line represents the envelope of a wave packet.
