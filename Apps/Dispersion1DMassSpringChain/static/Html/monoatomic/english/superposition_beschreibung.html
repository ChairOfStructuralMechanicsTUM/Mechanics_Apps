Superposition of Two Monoatomic Normal Modes

Reminder: Fundamentals of Dispersion

The phase velocity is defined as:

$$\upsilon_P = \frac{\omega}{k}$$

The group velocity is defined as:

$$\upsilon_G = \frac{\mathrm{d} \omega}{\mathrm{d} k}$$

In a non-dispersive medium (such as light in a vacuum), it holds: $$ \frac{\mathrm{d} \omega}{\mathrm{d} k} = \frac{\omega}{k}$$. In this case, all waves propagate at the same constant speed regardless of their wavelength $$\lambda$$.
Consequently, all wave pulses maintain their form.
In contrast, in a dispersive medium: $$ \frac{\mathrm{d} \omega}{\mathrm{d} k} \neq \frac{\omega}{k}$$. In this case, the individual phases of a wave packet have a phase velocity that is dependent on their wave vector (or wavelength) and differs from the group velocity of the wave packet.
As a result, the individual phases of the wave packet propagate at different speeds and the shape of a single, isolated wave packet disperses over time.
For this reason, the ratio between $$\omega$$ and $$k$$ is referred to as the dispersion ratio.

Animation of the Superposition of Two Normal Modes

Using the following tool, two normal modes $$\mu_1=k_1a$$ and $$\mu_2=k_2a$$ can be superimposed from the reduced 1. Brillouin zone. The selected normal modes are visualized in the above plot. The same experimental parameters as previously assumed are used for the animation.
The displacement of the masses resulting from the superposition is visualised in an animation of the mass-spring chain. The graph below shows the displacements of the individual masses with respect to their rest position. The x-axis corresponds to the positions of the initial locations of the individual masses. The equilibrium positions of the individual mass points are plotted as points on the graph. The y-axis corresponds to the longitudinal displacement of the masses. A positive displacement corresponds to a displacement of the mass to the right.
The phase and group velocity of the superposition are represented in the plot as red and black dots, respectively.
The solid line represents the resulting wave from the superposition (referred to hereafter as the carrier wave), while the dashed line visualizes the envelope of a wave packet.
