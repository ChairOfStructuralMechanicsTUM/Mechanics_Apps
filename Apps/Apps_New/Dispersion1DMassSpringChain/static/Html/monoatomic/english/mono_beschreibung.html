Derivation of the Dispersion Relation

The equation of motion for the mass $$m_n$$ is given by:

$$m_n \ddot{u}_n = c (u_{n+1}-2 u_n + u_{n-1})$$

Due to the periodicity of the system, the Bloch theorem can be employed to derive the eigenvalue problem $$\omega(k)$$ based on a single unit cell. For this purpose, a harmonic, plane wave is assumed as a solution to the homogeneous differential equation.
Ansatz:

$$u(x_n,t)=u_n(t)=\hat{u}(\omega)e^{-i\omega t}=A(k(\omega))e^{i(ka-\omega t)}$$

Here, $$k$$ represents the Bloch wave vector and is defined as: $$k=\frac{2\pi n}{Na}$$.
$$n$$ denotes the numbering of a unit cell and $$N$$ represents the total number of unit cells within a segment of the periodic structure, fulfilling periodic boundary conditions with the Bloch theorem.
Using the Bloch theorem, $$\hat{u}(\omega)$$ describes the expression of phase change from one unit cell to the next adjacent unit cell.

$$\hat{u}(\omega)=A(k(\omega))e^{ika}$$

Furthermore, the definition of the dimensionless wave number $$\mu=\frac{2\pi}{Na}a$$ is adopted. Thus, the approach simplifies to:

$$u_n(t)=A(k(\omega))e^{i(\mu n - \omega t)}$$

Substituting into the differential equation and considering the relationship $$2cos(x) = e^{ix}+e^{-ix}$$, we obtain:

$$Ae^{i(\mu n-\omega t)} [-\omega^2m + 2c(1-\cos{\mu})] = 0$$

The non-trivial solution of the differential equation yields the dispersion relation $$\omega(k)$$ of the monoatomic mass-spring chain:

$$\omega(k) = \sqrt{\frac{4c}{m}} | \frac{sin(ka)}{2}| = \sqrt{\frac{4c}{m}} | \frac{sin(\mu)}{2}|$$
