Superposition of Two Diatomic Normal Modes

Reminder: Fundamentals of Dispersion

The phase velocity is defined as:

$$\upsilon_P = \frac{\omega}{k}$$

The group velocity is defined as:

$$\upsilon_G = \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial k}$$

In a non-dispersive medium (light in vacuum), it holds that: $$ \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial k} = \frac{\omega}{k}$$. In this case, all waves propagate with the same constant velocity.
Consequently, all wave pulses (superposition of normal modes) maintain their form.
In contrast, in a dispersive medium: $$ \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial k} \neq \frac{\omega}{k}$$. In this case, individual phases have a phase velocity dependent on their wave vector (or wavelength), which differs from the group velocity of the wave packet.
Consequently, the individual phases of a superimposed wave propagate at different speeds, causing the form of an isolated wave packet to disperse over time.
For this reason, the ratio between $$\omega$$ and $$k$$ is referred to as the dispersion ratio.

Animation of Superposition of Two Normal Modes

With the aid of the following tool, two normal modes $$\mu_1=k_1a$$ and $$\mu_2=k_2a$$ within a frequency range from the reduced 1st Brillouin zone can be superimposed. The selected wave vectors are visualized in the above illustration.
The same experimental parameters as previously assumed are employed for the animation.
The displacement of the masses resulting from the superposition is visualized both in an animation of the mass-spring chain and as a transverse wave in the plot below.
The x-axis corresponds to the positions of the initial positions of the individual masses. The equilibrium positions of the individual mass points are plotted in the graph as a blue point for the mass $$m_1$$ and as a gray point for the mass $$m_2$$.
The y-axis corresponds to the longitudinal displacement of the masses. A positive displacement corresponds to a displacement of the mass to the right.
The phase and group velocities of the superposition are depicted in the plot as a red and black point, respectively.
The solid blue ($$m_1$$) or gray line ($$m_2$$) represents the resulting wave from the superposition (hereafter referred to as the carrier wave), while the dashed line represents the envelope of a wave packet.
